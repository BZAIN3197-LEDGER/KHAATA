How KHAATA helps you plan

A useful budget starts with the money actually available, gives necessary expenses first consideration, and makes trade-offs visible. KHAATA is designed to help you organize that process rather than assume every household should spend in the same proportions.

1. Describe your situation

Choose a country and city, set the budget period and available amount, and enter the household members included in the plan.

2. Review the proposed plan

Examine the suggested spending categories, amounts, and explanations. Treat estimates as a starting point and compare them with your real bills and local costs.

3. Adjust priorities

Consider essential food, housing, utilities, transport, education and healthcare before flexible spending. Include emergency savings when your circumstances allow.

Four principles for a more realistic budget

  1. Start with actual obligations. Use rent, utility bills, school fees, transport costs, debt payments and other known commitments where available instead of relying entirely on generic percentages.
  2. Separate needs from flexible spending. Essential costs vary between households. Review the consequences before cutting food, medicine, education or other necessary expenses.
  3. Make room for irregular expenses. Annual fees, repairs, medical costs and seasonal bills can disrupt a monthly plan. Estimate them and set aside money over time where possible.
  4. Review and update. Compare planned amounts with actual spending at the end of the period. Change the next budget when prices, income or household needs change.

Frequently asked budgeting questions

Does KHAATA guarantee that the budget will match local prices?

No. A generated plan is an estimate and may not reflect every current price or personal obligation. Check important amounts against your bills, local providers and other reliable information.

What if my essential expenses are higher than my available money?

The budget should make the shortfall visible rather than hide it. Review flexible spending, identify any safe adjustments, and consider trusted local assistance or qualified advice if essential needs cannot be covered.

Should every household use the same spending percentages?

No. A percentage-based example can help explain a budget, but rent, family size, income stability, health needs, location and existing obligations all affect a realistic allocation.

Is KHAATA a financial adviser?

No. KHAATA is a budgeting aid. Its output can contain errors and should not replace professional financial, legal, tax or debt advice when that advice is needed.
