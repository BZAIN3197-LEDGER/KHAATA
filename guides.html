Budgeting Guides

A budget helps you understand where your available money needs to go. These guides cover common budgeting situations and practical ways to organize expenses.

How to Create a Monthly Family Budget

Start by calculating the total money available for the month. List essential expenses such as housing, food, utilities, transportation and healthcare.

After essential expenses have been considered, set aside an amount for savings or unexpected costs when possible. The remaining money can then be allocated to less essential spending according to the household's priorities.

Building an Emergency Fund

An emergency fund is money reserved for unexpected expenses such as urgent repairs, medical costs or a temporary reduction in income.

The appropriate amount depends on household income, expenses and circumstances. Even small regular contributions can gradually create a financial buffer.

How to Manage Food Spending

Food spending varies according to household size, location, dietary choices and shopping habits.

Planning meals, comparing prices, buying commonly used items efficiently and reducing food waste can help households understand and manage this category.

Budgeting for Education

Education expenses can include school fees, books, transportation, uniforms, supplies, tuition and other recurring or occasional costs.

Listing these expenses separately can make it easier to understand how much education contributes to the overall household budget.

Managing a Limited Budget

When available money is limited, essential expenses generally need to be considered before discretionary spending.

Reviewing recurring subscriptions, unnecessary purchases and other flexible expenses can help identify areas where spending could potentially be adjusted.

Understanding the 50/30/20 Rule

The 50/30/20 rule is a commonly discussed budgeting framework that divides after-tax income between needs, wants and savings or debt repayment.

It is a framework rather than a universal requirement. Housing costs, income, family size and local living expenses can make different allocations more appropriate for different households.
